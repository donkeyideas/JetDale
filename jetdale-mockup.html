PawPair

Trusted dog sitters, three doors down.

The problem

Dog owners in dense urban neighborhoods don't trust strangers with their pets, but they also can't afford boarding ($75/night avg in NYC) or won't drop their dog at a kennel. They want someone local — ideally a neighbor — who they have already met or who comes with a strong recommendation from someone they know.

The vision

A trust-first sitter marketplace where every match is verified by a real connection — a neighbor, a vet, a previous client — not just a star rating.

Why now

  • Pet ownership in cities is up 41% since 2019 — the post-pandemic shift hasn't reversed.
  • Rover and Wag treat sitters as gig workers; trust is downstream of price.
  • Neighborhood-graph apps (Nextdoor, Citizen) have normalized hyperlocal identity.
  • Consumers are paying premium for "verified human" services — see Cameo, the rise of curated marketplaces.

Target user — primary persona

PainHas cancelled trips to avoid leaving her dog with strangers
Job to be done"Find someone I'd trust with my house keys, not just my dog"

Demographics22-35, works from home, owns a dog, urban

Job to be done"Get paid for what I'd already do — hang with dogs"

Success criteria for Year 1

  • Launch in 3 neighborhoods (target: Williamsburg, Park Slope, LES) by month 4.
  • 500 verified sitters across launch markets, each with 3+ vouches.
  • 4,000 completed bookings, 4.85+ avg rating, <2% incident rate.
  • $120K GMV in Q4, 18% take rate, path-to-profitability slide for seed raise.
